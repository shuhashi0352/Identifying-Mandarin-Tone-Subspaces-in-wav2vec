This is exactly the distinction:

“Highly decodable” ≠ “aligned with top PCA geometry”

Your probe showed:
	•	tone is clearly there

Your PCA sweep showed:
	•	but not in a tiny dominant variance subspace

That means decodability and geometric prominence are different things.

This is a central result.

Tone is one important factor, but not the sole largest source of variance.

Although tone is highly decodable from Layer 6 wav2vec representations (94.4%), the top principal components alone perform near chance, indicating that tone information is distributed across multiple dimensions rather than concentrated in the dominant variance directions.

## What this means for PCA vs DAS framing

This result motivates your causal-subspace idea.

Because if top PCA directions miss tone:
	•	PCA may not isolate the functionally relevant subspace efficiently.

That is exactly why your project is interesting.
