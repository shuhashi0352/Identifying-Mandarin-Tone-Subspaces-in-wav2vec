# More explained variance does NOT monotonically improve classification.

That is the main finding.

Even though 2 PCs explain 96% of variance, they perform worse than 1 PC.

That means:

Some high-variance directions are not useful for tone labels and may interfere with linear classification.



# Very interesting surprise: k=1 is strong

One PC alone = 53.6%

That means a single dominant contour factor carries a lot of tone information.

Likely PC1 reflects something like:
	•	global pitch height + contour direction

This is huge.

One scalar already beats many baselines you might expect.


# Why k=2 to k=4 dips

Likely the added PCs encode variation such as:
	•	speaker residual style
	•	syllable-specific articulation
	•	local noise
	•	interpolation variance

These dimensions add variance but blur class boundaries.


# Why k=9/10 recovers

Once nearly all dimensions are restored, logistic regression can combine them and recover the original useful structure.

So the “bad middle zone” is:
	•	too compressed to preserve all useful cues
	•	but includes some nuisance axes


Strong interpretation sentence

Tone-discriminative information is distributed across the contour space in a way that is not perfectly aligned with variance-ranked principal components. 
High explained variance alone is therefore insufficient to guarantee optimal classification performance.



The F0 analysis you just finished gives you a baseline for what simple contour geometry can do. Now you want to ask:
	•	how well do hidden states classify tone?
	•	how much of that is captured by top PCA directions?
	•	does wav2vec PCA behave like F0 PCA, or differently?
